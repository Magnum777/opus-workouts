## Pre-History & The Dark Ages

### AD 7987 — The EVE Gate Opens
- **What happened:** A natural wormhole is discovered on the astral frontier of human space. The colonized universe is plagued by overpopulation and resource scarcity. A team of scientists and specialists from several nations mounts the first manned expedition. On the other side: several habitable worlds ripe for colonization.
- **Why it matters:** This is the origin of all human civilization in New Eden. The leader of the expedition declared humanity "on the eve of a second genesis" — the name "EVE" stuck.
- **Story hook:** A secret research vessel left behind a data cache in New Eden just before the collapse. What if someone finds it?

### AD ~7987–8061 — The Colonization Period
- **What happened:** Mass colonization of the New Eden cluster. Gigantic construction ships build star gates, allowing smaller vessels to follow. Three generations of expansion. A religious sect called the Conformists (led by Dano Gheinok) names the system "New Eden."
- **Why it matters:** The Conformists would later become the foundation of Amarr religious thought.
- **Story hook:** A colony ship that never arrived at its destination suddenly appears at a modern stargate. Everyone on board is dead — but their ship logs tell a different story.

### AD 8061, February 20 — The EVE Gate Collapses
- **What happened:** A patrol ship reports gravitational fluctuations and sensor interference near the EVE Gate. A deafening hiss overtakes comms channels. A cataclysmic energy blast spirals from the wormhole, annihilating everything nearby. The wormhole collapses into a dead singularity.
- **Why it matters:** Every colony in New Eden is instantly cut off from the Milky Way — and from each other. The Dark Ages begin. Millions perish.
- **Story hook:** A Jove scientist claims to have found a signal emanating from the dead singularity. Is the gate truly dead?

### AD 8061 – ~21000 — The Dark Ages
- **What happened:** Panic, riots, civil war. Terraforming stops. Equipment falls into ruin. Colonies lose contact with each other. Knowledge is lost. Most colonies die out. Only a few resilient pockets survive, passing down basic trades through generations.
- **Why it matters:** This is why New Eden civilizations are so different from each other — they evolved in isolation for thousands of years.
- **Story hook:** A derelict station from the Dark Ages is found in an uncharted system. Its inhabitants survived — but they've become something else entirely.

## Rebirth & First Contact

### AD ~21000 – 22480 — The Reawakening
- **What happened:** Gradually, colonies on shared planets rediscover each other. Space exploration technology is reinvented. New star gates are built. Planets are settled anew. Five distinct civilizations emerge: Amarr, Caldari, Gallente, Minmatar, and Jove.
- **Why it matters:** The modern empires are born from the ashes of the Dark Ages.
- **Story hook:** Two rival factions claim descent from the same pre-collapse colony. Their ancient grudge reignites a war.

### AD 22480 — Amarr Conquest of Matar
- **What happened:** Emperor Damius III orders the full conquest and enslavement of the Minmatar people. The Minmatar Empire effectively ceases to exist. Every remaining colony world is invaded. Hundreds of millions of Minmatar are shipped across the Amarr Empire as slaves.
- **Why it matters:** This defines the Amarr-Minmatar relationship for the next 700+ years. The Minmatar become a slave race under Amarr rule.
- **Story hook:** A Minmatar slave discovers they are secretly of Starkmanir descent — the tribe the Amarr thought they had exterminated.

### AD ~22966 — The Starkmanir Rebellion
- **What happened:** ~450 years after conquest, the Starkmanir tribe rises up. The rebellion is so serious that a royal heir of House Ardishapur is killed. In response, the Amarr order the Starkmanir annihilated by orbital bombardment. The genocide is almost complete — but not quite.
- **Why it matters:** The khumaak, the symbol of Starkmanir resistance, spreads among all Minmatar. The dream of freedom is born.
- **Story hook:** A Starkmanir survivor's descendant is discovered living on an Amarr agricultural world. What happens when the truth comes out?

### AD 23121 — The Gallente Federation Founded
- **What happened:** The interstellar union of the Gallente people is formally created, marking the beginning of the Luminate Era.
- **Why it matters:** The Gallente become the democratic counterweight to the authoritarian Amarr and corporate Caldari.
- **Story hook:** A historical archive is uncovered showing the Gallente founding fathers secretly traded Minmatar refugees to the Amarr in exchange for peace.

### AD ~23150s — The Caldari Secede / Gallente-Caldari War Begins
- **What happened:** The Caldari corporations, already powerful within Gallente society, secede from the Federation. The Gallente-Caldari War erupts — a conflict that would last nearly a century and be fought largely to a stalemate.
- **Why it matters:** This is the foundational trauma of the Caldari State. They define themselves against the Gallente.
- **Story hook:** A retired Gallente admiral and a Caldari CEO meet at a neutral station, both seeking the same lost ship from the war's early days.

### BYC20 (AD 23216) — Battle of Vak'Atioth & The Great Rebellion
- **What happened:** The Amarr send 200 warships to demonstrate their might against the mysterious Jove Empire. The Jovians annihilate them — the first crushing defeat of an Amarr fleet by another race. As news spreads, the Minmatar resistance sends the signal to rise up. The Great Rebellion begins. The Minmatar eject the Amarr from Pator and many systems, establishing the Minmatar Republic. The Amarr are forced to sue for peace with the Jove while fire spreads through their empire.
- **Why it matters:** Two pivotal events at once — the Amarr learn they are not invincible, and the Minmatar win their freedom. The modern balance of power is established.
- **Story hook:** A Jove veteran of Vak'Atioth appears in a Minmatar bar, carrying a data core that proves the Jove deliberately provoked the Amarr into attacking.

## The Modern Era (YC Calendar)

### YC0 (AD 23236) — The Yoiul Conference & CONCORD
- **What happened:** The five empires (Amarr, Caldari, Gallente, Minmatar, Jove) gather aboard the Jove cruiser *Yoiul* and establish CONCORD — the Consolidated Cooperation and Relations Command. The YC (Yoiul Conference) calendar is adopted as universal time.
- **Why it matters:** CONCORD becomes the (uneasy) arbiter of interstellar peace. The empires agree to limited cooperation — but tensions remain.
- **Story hook:** The original Yoiul Conference treaties contain a secret clause, redacted from all public records, that the Jove included as insurance.

### YC ~3–5 — Gallente-Caldari War Ends (Treaty)
- **What happened:** After nearly a century of warfare, the first Gallente-Caldari War ends in a stalemate. Both sides agree to a peace treaty. The Caldari State retains its independence.
- **Why it matters:** The war never truly ends in the hearts of either people. It merely goes cold.
- **Story hook:** A peace treaty document is discovered with an addendum neither side ever ratified — an addendum that could restart the war.

### YC ~8 — Jove Gift the Capsule Technology
- **What happened:** The Jovian Directorate gifts a small Caldari megacorporation with blueprints for the hydrostatic capsule — a technology that allows a human consciousness to interface directly with a starship and survive death via clone reactivation.
- **Why it matters:** This is the technology that makes capsuleers possible. The Jove are ~120 years ahead of other races; why they shared this remains debated.
- **Story hook:** The Caldari corporation that received the gift went bankrupt decades ago. Its founder's personal journal — containing the true reason the Jove shared the tech — is up for auction.

## The Capsuleer Age

### YC105 — The Dawn of the Independent Capsuleer
- **What happened:** The capsule technology becomes widespread enough that independent capsuleers — immortals operating outside empire control — begin appearing in significant numbers. CONCORD faces its greatest challenge: crowd control of an elite class that cannot be permanently killed.
- **Why it matters:** The capsuleer is the player character. This is when "the game" truly begins in lore terms. Power shifts from empires to these immortal individuals and the alliances they form.
- **Story hook:** One of the first capsuleers ever created is found in cryosleep, their clone network offline for a century. They wake up to a galaxy they don't recognize.

### YC111, March 10 — The Seyllin Disaster
- **What happened:** A catastrophic stellar event in the Seyllin system (Gallente Federation) destroys the planet Seyllin I and kills millions. The disaster is later understood to be connected to a broader network of unstable wormholes — the first major sign that something is changing in the fabric of space.
- **Why it matters:** This is the first of the "Seven Events of the Apocalypse" prophesied by Dr. Damella Macaper. It marks the beginning of the Age of Crises.
- **Story hook:** A survivor of Seyllin I is discovered in an escape pod — but they shouldn't exist, because no escape pods made it out. Their DNA doesn't match any known database.

### YC116, December — Caroline's Star & The Thera Incident
- **What happened:** A superluminal stellar anomaly appears in the W477-P system, nicknamed "Caroline's Star" after a Sisters of EVE researcher. Simultaneously, the Thera system — a nexus of unstable wormholes — is discovered. CONCORD issues an emergency statement. The Sisters of EVE lose contact with a wormhole colony.
- **Why it matters:** Caroline's Star is the visible manifestation of something happening deep in Sleeper/Drifter space. It's the harbinger of the Drifter emergence.
- **Story hook:** A CONCORD analyst who worked on the Caroline's Star report goes missing. Their last message: "It's not a star. It's a door."

### YC116–117 — The Drifters Emerge
- **What happened:** Mysterious vessels of unknown origin — the Drifters — begin appearing across New Eden. They possess technology far beyond empire capabilities and seem to be searching for something. They are connected to the ancient Sleeper civilization but are not Sleepers themselves.
- **Why it matters:** The Drifters represent the first major extragalactic(?) threat since the Jove revelation. Their technology and motives remain largely unknown.
- **Story hook:** A capsuleer boards a drifting Drifter hulk and finds a human corpse inside — a corpse that shouldn't exist, wearing a uniform from a fleet that vanished in the Gallente-Caldari War.

## The Age of Crises

### YC118–122 — The Triglavian Invasion
- **What happened:** The Triglavian Collective — a civilization from an unknown region of space called "Abyssal Deadspace" — begins incursions into known space. They deploy strange technology, harvest stellar resources, and test the empires' defenses. Over months, the invasion escalates through three chapters, culminating in a full-scale push into empire space.
- **Why it matters:** The Triglavians represent a completely alien civilization with its own logic and philosophy. They force the empires and capsuleers to cooperate — or choose sides.
- **Story hook:** A Triglavian communication is translated incorrectly by CONCORD. The real meaning is far more terrifying — and a lone capsuleer is the only one who knows.

### YC122, October — Pochven Formation (Totality Day)
- **What happened:** The Triglavian Invasion reaches its zenith. Using massive World Ark vessels, the Triglavians sever dozens of star systems from empire space and merge them into a new region: **Pochven**, the Triglavian-controlled domain. The boundaries of known space are permanently redrawn. The event becomes known as "Totality Day."
- **Why it matters:** Pochven is now a no-man's land between the empires and the Triglavians. Billions of people are trapped inside. The political map of New Eden changes forever.
- **Story hook:** A family trapped in Pochven since the formation sends a message out through a dead-drop courier network. They claim the Triglavians are not conquering — they are *recruiting*.

## The Current Era (YC122–YC128)

### YC122–YC126 — The Pochven Stalemate
- **What happened:** The Triglavians consolidate their hold on Pochven. CONCORD establishes limited diplomatic contact. Capsuleers operate in the region as mercenaries, traders, and explorers. The empires slowly adapt to a universe where a new power bloc exists alongside them.
- **Why it matters:** New Eden is no longer a four-empire system. It's five empires, plus capsuleer alliances, plus rogue factions — all in uneasy tension.
- **Story hook:** A Pochven native who has never known empire rule is rescued by a capsuleer. Their culture shock is mutual — and dangerous.

### YC127 — The Warpath Protocol Failure
- **What happened:** CONCORD's war response protocols — the systems designed to prevent all-out war between the empires — suffer a catastrophic failure. Empire conflicts escalate dramatically, with fighting spilling into high-security space that was once sacrosanct.
- **Why it matters:** The one thing keeping the empires from total war is broken. The rules of engagement no longer apply.
- **Story hook:** A CONCORD DED agent discovers the protocol failure was not an accident. Someone — or something — deliberately sabotaged the system.

### YC128, April — Empire Conflict Escalates
- **What happened:** The Caldari State grants CONCORD administrative oversight of the Jita system — the trade hub of New Eden. CONCORD and AIR announce the establishment of a new space region: **Exordium**, accessible through a Yulai Accord amendment. The empires are effectively at war in high-sec space.
- **Why it matters:** Jita under CONCORD administration is a seismic shift in the economic and political landscape. Exordium represents new frontiers — and new battlegrounds.
- **Story hook:** A Jita station administrator finds a dead drop containing evidence that the Caldari ceded Jita not willingly, but because something worse is coming that they need CONCORD's help to stop.

### YC128, April — Alix Moreau Elected President of the Gallente Federation
- **What happened:** Alix Moreau wins the Gallente presidential election with 34.8% of the popular vote, defeating Soraya Roden by one district. The election is contentious and divisive.
- **Why it matters:** The Gallente Federation is politically divided at a time when external threats are escalating. Leadership matters.
- **Story hook:** Moreau's victory was helped by a single district where voter turnout was statistically impossible. A hacker claims they rigged it — and they have proof Moreau knew.
