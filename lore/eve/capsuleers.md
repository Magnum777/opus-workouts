# Capsuleers

## Overview

A capsuleer—also called a capsule pilot or pod pilot—is a human who commands a starship through a **hydrostatic capsule** and has been rendered effectively immortal through **cloning technology**. They are the player characters of EVE Online, but within the lore they represent something far more profound: a new class of posthuman demigods who have reshaped the political, economic, and military landscape of New Eden.

The capsuleer age began in early **YC 104** (roughly 30 years before the present day), when the union of the hydrostatic capsule and the transneural burn scanner was perfected. Since then, capsuleers have grown from a rare elite into a dominant force—immortal mercenaries, empire-builders, traders, and warlords who operate largely outside the control of the four great empires.

## The Capsule Technology

### The Hydrostatic Capsule

The **hydrostatic capsule** is a sealed, armored shell filled with a breathable, high-pressure liquid suspension. The pilot inside is wired directly into the ship's systems through a neural interface, allowing them to perceive and control the vessel as an extension of their own body. This direct neural link far outperforms traditional bridge command, where orders are relayed through a chain of officers.

The capsule provides:
- **Direct neural control** of all ship systems (weapons, propulsion, sensors, defenses)
- **Sensory translation** of the ship's sensor data into perceptible experience
- **FTL communication** integrated with the New Eden stargate network
- **A sealed, controlled environment** with precise tolerances for life-support and scanning systems

When a capsule breaches without authorization, the system assumes catastrophe and administers a **nanotoxin** that produces a reliable, instant death. This is not a bug—it is the critical feature that enables immortality.

### The Transneural Burn Scanner

The second cornerstone of capsuleer immortality is the **transneural burn scanner**. Developed in YC 104, this device takes a complete, accurate snapshot of the pilot's entire mind-brain state and transmits it nearly instantaneously to a waiting clone body.

The scanner must fire within a window of nanoseconds at the moment of death. Firing too early corrupts the scan; firing too late captures a dying, damaged brain. The capsule's sealed environment and controlled breach parameters make this timing possible. When the nanotoxin kills the pilot, the scanner fires, and consciousness transfers seamlessly to the clone.

The process destroys the original brain—it is a one-way transfer. The philosophical implications have been debated for decades: Is the clone the same person? Most capsuleers answer pragmatically: they remember, they think, they persist. That is enough.

## Cloning and Consciousness Transfer

### How It Works

1. A capsuleer maintains an active **clone contract** with a licensed cloning facility.
2. The clone body is kept in stasis, genetically identical to the original, with neural architecture prepared to receive a scan.
3. When the pilot dies (usually via capsule breach and nanotoxin), the burn scanner transmits the mind-state via FTL comms.
4. The clone is activated with the pilot's complete memories, personality, and consciousness intact.
5. The pilot "wakes up" in a new body, often light-years away from where they died.

### Limitations and Risks

- **Scan corruption**: If the scanner fires at the wrong moment, the resulting consciousness may be damaged, psychologically unstable, or reduced to autonomic function.
- **Clone degradation**: Repeated cloning can cause psychological drift. Some capsuleers report "not feeling like themselves" after many deaths.
- **Cost**: Clone contracts and jump clones are expensive. Losing a pod in hostile space without an updated clone can result in true death—or worse, a corrupted revival.
- **Alpha clones**: A simpler, limited form of cloning developed by the Sisters of EVE, allowing broader access to capsule technology without full immortality.

### Cultural Attitudes

Cloning was once fiercely demonized by religious groups across the cluster, seen as a denial of the human soul. Even today it is not universally accepted. The Amarr Empire views capsuleers with suspicion, seeing their immortality as an affront to God's plan. The Minmatar Republic, shaped by the trauma of slavery, has a more pragmatic view—capsuleers are tools of liberation. The Gallente Federation celebrates the individual transcendence. The Caldari State sees capsuleers as economic and military assets to be exploited.

## Becoming a Capsuleer

Pod pilot candidates are extraordinarily rare. Of all applicants:
- **14%** make it past prescreening into basic training
- **~5%** of those complete the full program
- **Roughly 0.7%** of all applicants become capsuleers

### Requirements

- 20/20 vision, perfect hearing
- Blood pressure within highly limited range
- Peak physical conditioning (able to run 60+ kilometers without pause)
- Complete genome profile excluding all genetic defects or hereditary disease
- 98th percentile intelligence
- Advanced technical degree
- Enormous financial resources (unless sponsored by grants or scholarships)
- Above all: the ability to keep multiple mental tracks active simultaneously, giving full attention to all

### Training (5 Years)

| Phase | Duration | Content |
|-------|----------|---------|
| **Academic** | 1–2 years | Capsule and cloning theory, ship systems, crew management, maintenance. Culminates in a 6-hour oral exam. |
| **Physical Orientation** | 2 years | Deprivation tanks, zero-G acclimation, warp and wormhole shock simulation. Highest failure and mortality rate (~10% of all candidates die here). |
| **Flight Training** | 2 years | Hardpoint configuration, warp mechanics, shields, signature analysis, astrometrics. First year: simulations. Second year: real capsule-fitted frigates. |
| **Final Trial** | — | **Voluntary euthanasia.** The candidate must submit to death by nanotoxin and clone into a new body for the first time. Many cannot cross this Rubicon. |

## CONCORD Regulation

### CONCORD Assembly

The **Consolidated Cooperation and Relations Command Assembly** (CONCORD) is the independent organization that licenses, monitors, and—attempts to—regulate capsuleers. Founded over a century ago to mediate between the empires, CONCORD has grown into a powerful force in its own right, funded by customs, confiscations, license sales, and infrastructure management.

CONCORD's relationship with capsuleers is complex:
- They **license** capsuleers as independent pilots
- They **enforce** certain laws in high-security space (instant response to illegal aggression)
- They **cannot** control capsuleer activity in low-security or null-security space
- They **facilitate** proxy warfare between empires using capsuleer militias

### Directive Enforcement Department (DED)

The **DED** is CONCORD's police force and maintains the strongest fleet in New Eden. Their responsibilities include:
- Tracking high-profile criminals
- Licensing bounty hunters
- Patrolling smuggling routes
- Providing security at empire summits
- Rapid response to illegal aggression in highsec

The DED's special forces unit, **SARO** (Special Affairs for Regulations and Order), handles hostage situations, tactical assaults on pirate havens, and high-risk engagements.

### CONCORD Emergency Militia War Powers Act

In YC 110, following the Elder War and the crippling of CONCORD's rapid-response infrastructure, the assembly passed the **CONCORD Emergency Militia War Powers Act**. This legislation empowered capsuleers to form militias and fight on behalf of the four empires over contested territory—formalizing the **Faction Warfare** system.

The Act was a concession to reality: CONCORD could not control capsuleers, so it channeled them. Today, capsuleer militias fight proxy wars in the warzones under CONCORD's watchful but distant gaze.

### CONCORD's Limits

CONCORD does not protect players—it punishes lawbreaking. In highsec, a criminal act triggers an inevitable response: CONCORD ships warp in, apply overwhelming EWAR and firepower, and destroy the offender. But the response takes time (6–19 seconds depending on security level), and CONCORD will not intervene until after the first illegal shot is fired.

This creates the infamous **suicide ganking** dynamic: criminals accept death-by-CONCORD as a cost of doing business, striking for profit before the inevitable response.

## Capsuleer Culture

### Immortal Demigods

To baseline humans, capsuleers are terrifying. They do not stay dead. They command ships worth billions. They can destroy fleets, collapse markets, or conquer star systems. They are worshipped, feared, hired, and hated.

Common perceptions:
- **Mercenaries**: Guns for hire, loyal only to the highest bidder
- **Demigods**: Above mortal law, beyond mortal consequence
- **Empire-Builders**: The true rulers of nullsec, where CONCORD cannot reach
- **Economic Titans**: Market manipulators who move trillions of ISK
- **Madmen**: Repeated death and cloning drive some to sociopathy

### The Empyrean Age

The period following YC 104 is often called the **Empyrean Age**—the age of the capsuleer. It brought:
- A boom in space technology and industry
- Rapid expansion of cloning facilities
- Enhanced FTL communications via the stargate network
- The rise of capsuleer-alliances as true political powers
- Proxy wars between empires fought by immortal pilots

## The Tension Between Empires and Capsuleers

### Fear and Exploitation

The empires need capsuleers. Their ships, their skill, their immortality make them invaluable military and economic assets. But the empires also fear them:

- **Amarr**: Views capsuleers as soulless abominations, yet relies on them for defense and expansion
- **Caldari**: Treats them as corporate assets, contracts them like any other resource
- **Gallente**: Celebrates their individual freedom but struggles to regulate their excesses
- **Minmatar**: Sees them as potential liberators but knows many are just profiteers

### Player-Formed Nations

In null-security space—where CONCORD does not go—capsuleers have built their own nations:
- Massive **alliances** controlling hundreds of star systems
- **Sov warfare**: Protracted wars over territorial sovereignty
- **Supercapital fleets**: Titan and supercarrier armadas that dwarf empire navies
- **Economic empires**: Player-owned markets, manufacturing chains, and resource extraction operations

These capsuleer nations are, in many ways, more powerful than the empires themselves in their domains. The great wars of nullsec—World War Bee, the Fountain War, the Casino War—have shaped the cluster's history as much as any empire conflict.

### Famous Capsuleer-Driven Events

| Event | Era | Significance |
|-------|-----|------------|
| **The Great Northern War** | Early Empyrean Age | Established early nullsec power blocs |
| **World War Bee / The Casino War** | YC118 | Largest war in EVE history; Goonswarm defeated and exiled, then returned |
| **The Fountain War** | YC115 | Defining conflict of modern nullsec sovereignty |
| **Burn Jita** | Recurring | Player-organized events demonstrating capsuleer power to disrupt even highsec |
| **The Battle of B-R5RB** | YC116 | Largest supercapital battle; trillions destroyed in a single day |
| **Faction Warfare Campaigns** | Ongoing | Capsuleer militias fighting empire proxy wars under CONCORD charter |
| **Drifter Crisis Response** | YC127 | Capsuleer fleets formed the frontline against the Drifter threat |
| **Triglavian Invasion Response** | YC121-122 | Capsuleers fought for and against the Triglavians, shaping Pochven's creation |

## Story Hooks

1. **The First Clone**: A candidate faces the final trial and hesitates. What does it mean to kill yourself and trust the copy?
2. **Corrupted Revival**: A capsuleer wakes up wrong—memories fragmented, personality altered. Is it still them?
3. **CONCORD's Dilemma**: An Inner Circle delegate must decide whether to legitimize a capsuleer warlord's claim to sovereignty—or try to crush them.
4. **The Alpha Clone Underground**: Unlicensed, limited clones are being distributed by criminal networks. Who is making them, and why?
5. **Amarr Heresy**: A capsuleer preacher claims immortality proves the Amarr faith wrong—and builds a cult in nullsec.
6. **The Burnout**: After 500 deaths in a year, a capsuleer starts refusing to reship. They sit in a station, terrified of the pod. Can they be helped?
7. **Empire Mercenary**: A capsuleer takes a Caldari State contract to destroy a Minmatar Republic fleet. Their family is on one of those ships.
8. **The Sleeper Connection**: A capsuleer starts experiencing dreams in the pod—memories that aren't theirs. Sleeper data? Or something else?
9. **Market War**: Two manufacturing empires wage economic war, hiring mercenaries, sabotaging supply chains, and moving trillions. The winner controls a tech II monopoly.
10. **The Nullsec Kingmaker**: A small alliance holds the tipping point in a war between superpowers. Every empire offers them a deal. Whichever they choose will reshape the cluster.
