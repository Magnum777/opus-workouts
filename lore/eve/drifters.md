# The Drifters

## Overview

The Drifters are a mysterious, highly advanced faction that emerged in New Eden in early YC117 (2015), seemingly from the ruins of the Sleeper civilization in Anoikis (W-space). They represent one of the most dangerous and least understood threats the cluster has ever faced. All attempts at communication have failed. Their technology rivals—and perhaps surpasses—that of the Jove, and they pursue their goals with implacable single-mindedness and utter ruthlessness.

## Who Are They?

### Jove Descendants—or Something Else?

By all appearances, the Drifters are members of the Jove race, but far removed and with strangely altered physiology. They exhibit an extremely high degree of cybernetic modification that seems tied to an alien mindset: cold, focused, and utterly without mercy. Unlike the relatively benign Jove Empire, the Drifters are aggressive, expansionist, and willing to wield overwhelming force against anyone in their way.

One speculation is that the Drifters are the inheritors of a legacy left by some of the most ancient Jove—possibly connected to the lost Second Jove Empire, which was erected by the so-called First Tyrant Miko Bour and later ruled by leaders known as Tyrants. The name "Tyrannos" (used by all Drifter commanders) points directly to this lineage.

### The Hive, the Other, and the Tyrannos

The Drifters operate under a single command structure: the **Vigilant Tyrannos**, an enclave-level organization led by **Tyrannos Strategos**. CONCORD intelligence has confirmed this is their unified military command, though no information on motivation or ultimate goals has been gleaned.

Within Sleeper lore, references exist to "the Hive" and "the Other"—an alien intelligence or AI presence that may influence or direct the Drifters. Some researchers speculate the Drifters are vessels for, or servants of, the Sleeper AI network known as the Other, while others believe they represent a competing or divergent branch of the same ancient civilization. The Drifters' harvesting of corpses (capsuleer, Jove, and Sleeper) suggests they may be using biomass and neural tissue for some unknown purpose—possibly cloning, possibly feeding an alien intelligence.

## The Seyllin Disaster and Caroline's Star

### The First Omen

The Drifters did not appear without warning. Their emergence was preceded by two phenomena:

1. **Jove Observatories** decloaking across known space—immense structures that had been hidden for decades, watching the empires. Many fell into disrepair.
2. **Circadian Seekers**—Sleeper scout drones—appearing throughout the cluster, scanning everything they encountered, including the observatories.

### Caroline's Star

The true herald of the Drifters was the **Caroline's Star** event. In YC116, a stellar anomaly was detected: the central star of the W477-P system in the UUA-F4 region underwent a cataclysmic stellar-level disruption of space-time. The Sisters of EVE confirmed this event caused the devastation of their colony in the Thera system (the "Thera Incident"). 

The Caroline's Star event was linked to the appearance of unstable wormholes, spatial rifts, and ultimately the Drifters themselves. It is believed to have been triggered by—or to have triggered—the activation of ancient Sleeper/Drifter technology on a stellar scale.

### The Seyllin Disaster (YC111)

Although predating the Drifters' visible emergence, the **Seyllin Disaster** of March 10, YC111 is widely seen as part of the same pattern of stellar manipulation. In the Seyllin system (Gallente Federation), the star ejected a massive plasma wave that obliterated Seyllin I, killing half a billion people. The star changed from a Type-O to a Type-A main sequence star. Many scientists believe the Seyllin event and Caroline's Star share a common origin in ancient technology designed to manipulate stars—technology the Drifters now control or seek.

## The Battle of Safizon (Amarr Navy Headquarters)

The Drifters announced themselves as a true threat with a devastating strike against the Amarr Empire.

### Assassination of Empress Jamyl I

Empress **Jamyl Sarum I**, ruler of the Amarr Empire from YC110 to YC117, was traveling aboard the Avatar-class titan **TES Seraph** during an inspection of the Amarr Navy Headquarters in **Safizon** when a powerful contingent of Drifter battleships ambushed and destroyed the vessel. The Empress was killed in the attack—assassinated by the Drifters in a stunning blow against the most powerful empire in New Eden.

The attack was not random. Jamyl I had previously returned from the dead (or so it seemed) and possessed knowledge and technology that may have made her a target. Her death shocked the cluster and prompted the Amarr Empire to declare war on the Drifters.

### The Drifter Advance on Safizon

Following the assassination, Drifter forces launched multiple assaults on Safizon and surrounding systems. The Imperial Navy fought back, with capsuleer militias and Amarr loyalist forces joining the defense. In one major engagement, Amarr coalition forces defeated a Drifter armada in Safizon, but at tremendous cost. Tens of thousands of navy personnel were killed, and the system was left littered with wreckage.

A second Drifter advance was pushed back after a 24-hour salvage and rescue operation, but the message was clear: the Drifters could strike anywhere, even the heart of an empire's military power.

## Recent Awakenings and Attacks

### The Drifter Crisis (YC127)

In YC127, the Drifters escalated dramatically. Drifter wormhole systems—previously inaccessible—were opened to capsuleer entry via new filaments. Inside, explorers found labyrinthine complexes, nexus points, and the **Warden**, a mysterious structure in Zarzakh that controls access to the Drifter hive systems.

Major developments included:
- **Reconfigured Hive Systems**: Drifter complexes transformed into labyrinthine constructs that partially exist in higher dimensions.
- **Nexus Points**: Mysterious structures advancing their own inscrutable machinations.
- **The "Race for the Hives"**: A cluster-wide scramble by empires and capsuleers to claim Drifter technology.

### The Five Occupation Zones

Five major factions established beachheads in Drifter wormhole systems:
- **Azdaja Redoubt** (J174618): Triglavian forces established a "Counter Domain"
- **Conflux Eyrie** (J200727): Caldari Navy base under Chairman Akimaka Saraki
- **Liberated Barbican** (J110145): Minmatar Republic Fleet task force led by Sanmatar Shakor
- **Sentinel MZ** (J055520): EDENCOM operational military zone under Provost Marshal Kasiha Valkanir
- **Sanctified Vidette** (J164710): Amarr Empire sanctified by Empress Catiz I herself

The Empress sanctified the system as a display of strength against the enemy that had murdered her predecessor. The Imperial Navy, Academy, and Theological Council all took up residence to study Drifter technology and its religious implications.

### The Crisis Resolution

By mid-YC127, reports indicated the Drifter Crisis had entered a new phase. The Society of Conscious Thought synthesized new upgrades for crisis response fleets using telemetry from destroyed Vigilant Dreamer structures. The Drifters were not invincible—but their full plans remained unknown.

### Tactics and Combat Behavior

- **Overshield**: An additional blue shield layer. When breached, target locks break (ECM-style effect) and the Drifter deploys its doomsday weapon.
- **Doomsday Weapon**: A devastating primary weapon (~750,000 damage) held in reserve. Fires when the overshield is depleted. Can be survived by extremely tanked capitals, fast small-signature orbiters, or ECM tactics.
- **Speed**: Surprisingly fast for their size. Can orbit at 2+ km/s.
- **Weaponry**: Lux Kontos suspended turret systems (semi-autonomous, omni-damage, ~1300 DPS standard). Secondary weapons are just as deadly.
- **Weakness**: Once shields are down, hull and armor offer little resistance. ECM is effective against their targeting.
- **Podding**: Unlike most NPCs, Drifters will pod capsuleers.
- **Aggression**: Attack if their sites are approached. Will pursue attackers. May warp in support (Lancers or other battleships).

### Engagement Strategies

1. **Bling Tank + Support**: Capitals or heavily tanked subcapitals (Paladin, Abaddon) with a support fleet of DPS and logi.
2. **Swarm Tactics**: A fleet of cheap, high-burst-DPS ships with webs. Fast and overwhelming, but expect losses.
3. **Speed Tank**: Fast cruisers with small signature radius (often with Halo implants), close orbit, tracking disruption, and web support. A fleet of 20-30 can take a Drifter down in seconds.

## Connections and Theories

- **Sleepers**: Drifters use Sleeper technology extensively. They appear alongside Sleeper installations in wormholes. Some speculate they prey upon Sleepers or represent a rival faction of the same civilization.
- **Jove**: Jove corpses float in Drifter wormhole systems. The Jove Observatories appear to be data-gathering posts the Drifters covet.
- **Sisters of EVE**: SOE ships appear in Drifter wormholes. The SOE's alpha clone technology may be connected to Drifter biology.
- **Deathless Circle**: In YC127, the Deathless Circle was reported to be in turmoil, scrambling to intercept remote Warden access—suggesting criminal interest in Drifter technology.

## Story Hooks

1. **The Warden's Purpose**: What is the Warden in Zarzakh, and why does it control access to the Hives? Is it a gatekeeper, a prison warden, or something else entirely?
2. **Corpse Harvesting**: Why do the Drifters harvest capsuleer, Jove, and Sleeper corpses? Are they cloning? Feeding an AI? Performing some ritual?
3. **The Other**: Is the Hive directed by the Sleeper AI known as the Other, or is the Other a separate entity in conflict with the Drifters?
4. **Empress Jamyl's Secret**: Jamyl I had returned from apparent death with strange knowledge. Did the Drifters kill her to silence her, or to reclaim something she possessed?
5. **Labyrinth Complexes**: The reconfigured Drifter complexes exist partially in higher dimensions. What happens if their "advancement" goes unchallenged?
6. **Triglavian-Drifter War**: The Triglavians call the Drifters "Azdaja" (Ancient Enemy). This ancient conflict predates New Eden's empires. What is its origin?
7. **The Tyrannos Clones**: Each Tyrannos name may represent a clone line. If identically named Tyrannoses are clones, how many are there, and where are they grown?
8. **Caroline's Star Revisited**: The stellar disruption in W477-P may have been intentional. Was it the Drifters' arrival point, their creation, or their weapon?
