# Fenris Creations Launches EVE's First Safe Zone; 23-Year Veterans Immediately Announce They're Moving to Star Citizen

**JITA, NEW EDEN** — The Cradle of War expansion launched yesterday to the sound of a million angry forum posts and at least one veteran ritualistically deleting their entire hangar, as Fenris Creations introduced Exordium, a 53-system region where, for the first time in EVE Online's 23-year history, players are not allowed to shoot each other.

The new player zone, which bans PvP entirely and replaces it with what developers describe as "tutorials," "mentorship," and "the radical concept of not exploding within the first five minutes," has been met with what can generously be described as a full-scale existential crisis among EVE's veteran population.

"I trashed all my stuff. You can't have it," wrote capsuleer Verlyn in a now-viral forum post titled simply "Lol, Safezones." The post, which accumulated dozens of likes before being buried under an avalanche of replies ranging from "can I have your stuff" to elaborate multi-paragraph eulogies for the game, has become the de facto flag under which disaffected veterans are rallying.

Verlyn, reached via in-game mail, elaborated: "I've been playing this game since 2003. I lost a Revelation dreadnought in 2008 because my cat walked across my keyboard. In 2014, my corp director emptied the wallet and bought 40,000 units of exotic dancers. I have a spreadsheet that tracks every ship I've ever lost, and it has 847 rows. This is not a game. This is a lifestyle. And now they're putting the new players in bubble wrap."

The Exordium zone, which creative director Bergur Finnbogason described at Fanfest as "a place where you should be moving out in a week, 10 days, two weeks," features deliberately terrible rewards and punitive taxes designed to push players out. This has not mollified critics.

"If the rewards are bad and the taxes are high, why not just let us shoot them?" asked veteran pilot Karnak7 in a forum thread that reached 40 pages within six hours. "The tax is supposed to be the punishment. I want the punishment to be my autocannons. I spent 12 years training those autocannons."

Several player corporations have announced boycotts. Goonfleet, never one to miss a propaganda opportunity, released a statement calling Exordium "the softest move in space MMO history" while simultaneously deploying recruiters to every Exordium system. Pandemic Horde reportedly held an emergency meeting about whether the safe zone constituted a strategic asset or an insult to their ancestors.

Fenris spokesperson Elara Voss addressed the backlash in a press conference that was interrupted three times by a player spamming the chat with ASCII art of a baby bottle.

"We understand the concern," Voss said. "But let's be clear: Exordium is 53 systems. EVE Online has 7,805 systems. You can still shoot people in 7,752 of them. That's a 99.3? shooting-to-not-shooting ratio, which we believe is acceptable even by the most bloodthirsty standards."

"The 0.7? of systems where you cannot commit murder," she added, "is approximately the same percentage of EVE players who have ever successfully completed the tutorial without rage-quitting."

New players, meanwhile, appear confused by the controversy. "I don't understand why everyone is so angry," said capsuleer Newt_Alpha, who joined three days ago and has so far completed two mining missions without being podded. "Isn't not dying the whole point?"

When informed that, historically, dying is in fact a significant portion of the EVE experience, Newt_Alpha paused. "Wait, so the veteran players are upset because they can't kill me? I'm literally in a training zone. This is like a professional boxer being angry that he can't punch someone at a Gold's Gym orientation."
