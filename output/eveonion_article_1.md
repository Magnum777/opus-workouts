# Six-Month EVE Online Player Wins $7,000 Ship From Free Loot Box, Immediately Asks If He Can Fit It In A Cormorant

**JITA** — The EVE Online community is reeling this week after a capsuleer with approximately six months of flight time pulled a Molok Titan blueprint from a free seasonal loot box, instantly acquiring an asset worth roughly 700 billion ISK and sparking what economists are calling "the most efficient wealth transfer from bitter veterans to a confused newbro in recorded history."

The player, who goes by the handle "xXDave_420Xx" and until last week believed that a Titan was "like a battleship but bigger, right?", reportedly opened the Crimson Harvest reward container while sitting undocked in a half-fit Cormorant outside Jita 4-4.

"Everyone gets this for free, right?" xXDave_420Xx allegedly typed into the Rookie Help channel, alongside a linked screenshot of the Molok blueprint. The channel, which typically handles questions about where to find Tritanium and why mining lasers won't fit on frigates, experienced what one moderator described as "a brief but total collapse of the social contract."

According to zKillboard data, only four Molok-class Titans have been destroyed since the hull was introduced in 2017 — a survival rate that Peter Farrell, EVE Online Community Manager and 20-year veteran, described as "genuinely absurd for a game where people blow up spaceships for fun." Farrell added that fewer than fifty pilots have ever recorded a kill while flying a Molok, making the ship "more of a museum piece that occasionally fires a doomsday weapon than an actual warship."

"The first thing you do when you get a Molok is you shoot your friend, or your alt, just to get your name in the record books," Farrell explained. "Dave here doesn't have an alt. He has a Velator he forgot to insure."

The economic shockwaves were immediate. Within hours of the pull being verified, veteran players across New Eden began performing what psychologists are calling "retrospective statistical trauma" — the rapid mental calculation of how many rats they've killed, how many sites they've run, and how many years they've spent accumulating wealth that a six-month player just received for clicking a box labeled "free stuff."

"I've been playing since 2006," said Kalden Rask, a nullsec veteran reached via comms in Pure Blind. "I own a Thanatos. One Thanatos. I have a spreadsheet for my spreadsheets. I calculate my ISK-per-hour while I sleep. And this guy — this absolute embryo of a capsuleer — just tripled my net worth by pressing F1 on a gift box." Rask then went silent for approximately forty-five seconds before adding, "I'm not mad. I'm just... I'm doing the math. That's all."

The HyperNet Relay, EVE's official in-game raffle system, has been at the center of renewed controversy following the pull. Critics argue the system constitutes gambling with extra steps, while supporters maintain it adds "high-stakes excitement" to the economy. At press time, a third faction had emerged arguing that nothing matters and the universe is fundamentally unjust, which most players noted was just EVE Online's normal philosophical baseline.

xXDave_420Xx wisely sold the blueprint rather than attempting to build or fly the Titan — a decision that industry veterans universally applauded since, as one manufacturer noted, "building a Titan requires resources, infrastructure, and organizational support that this man's current gameplay loop of 'lose three Cormorants per hour to gate rats' simply cannot provide."

The windfall means the newbie can now pay for his Omega subscription entirely with in-game ISK and has enough left over to "fly whatever he wants for years," according to market analysts. When asked about his plans for the wealth, xXDave_420Xx told reporters he was "thinking about trying a Catalyst, maybe."

"Honestly, it's just nice to not worry about ISK anymore," he added, before accidentally undocking in his pod and being destroyed by smartbombs on the Jita undock. He was able to afford a new clone.
