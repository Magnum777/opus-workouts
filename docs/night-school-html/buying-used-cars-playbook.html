Night School: Used Car Buying Guide

Topic: How to Buy a Used Car (Complete Playbook) Research Date: 2026-04-26 Source: CarEdge, CarGurus, Consumer Resources ---

Pre-Purchase Preparation

1. Set Your Budget (20/4/10 Rule)

  • 20% down payment minimum
  • 4 years max loan term (avoid 72+ month loans)
  • 10% of monthly gross income max for payment
  • Include: insurance, maintenance, fuel, registration

2. Check Your Credit Score

  • Higher scores = lower interest rates
  • Check free with Credit Karma, Experian, or bank app
  • Dispute errors before shopping

3. Get Pre-Approved for Financing

  • Shop rates at banks/credit unions first
  • Compare to dealer financing
  • Pre-approval = negotiating power
---

Research Phase

4. Identify Reliable Models

  • Check reliability ratings (Consumer Reports, J.D. Power)
  • Research common issues for specific year/model
  • Read owner forums for real-world problems

5. Research Market Value

  • Use CarEdge, CarGurus, KBB for price comparison
  • Check what others pay in your area
  • Know the fair price range before negotiating

6. Run Vehicle History Reports

  • Carfax or AutoCheck (free from many dealers)
  • Look for: accidents, title issues, odometer rollbacks
  • Verify service history if available
---

Inspection Checklist

Exterior Inspection

  • [ ] Paint match (signs of bodywork)
  • [ ] Panel gaps (uneven = collision repair)
  • [ ] Rust under doors, wheel wells, trunk
  • [ ] Tire tread depth (penny test)
  • [ ] All lights functional
  • [ ] Windshield cracks/chips

Interior Inspection

  • [ ] All electronics work (windows, locks, AC, heat)
  • [ ] No warning lights on dashboard
  • [ ] Seat adjustments functional
  • [ ] Odor (smoke, mold, chemicals)
  • [ ] Headliner condition
  • [ ] Trunk/cargo area clean

Under the Hood

  • [ ] Oil level and color (not black/burnt)
  • [ ] Coolant level and color
  • [ ] No leaks (oil, coolant, transmission)
  • [ ] Battery terminals clean
  • [ ] Belts not cracked

Test Drive

  • [ ] Cold start (any hesitation?)
  • [ ] Acceleration smooth
  • [ ] Brakes firm, no pulsing
  • [ ] Steering straight (no pull)
  • [ ] No unusual noises (knocks, squeals, rattles)
  • [ ] Transmission shifts smoothly
  • [ ] Cruise control works

The Deal

Negotiation Strategy

1. Start below asking (10-15% less)

2. Focus on out-the-door price (not monthly payment)

3. Be willing to walk away

4. Shop multiple dealers for same model

5. End of month/quarter = better deals

Required Documents from Seller

  • [ ] Title (clean, not salvage/rebuilt)
  • [ ] Registration
  • [ ] Maintenance records
  • [ ] Warranty docs (if any)

Before You Sign

  • [ ] Get Pre-Purchase Inspection by independent mechanic ($100-200)
  • [ ] Review all fees (doc fees, dealer prep, etc.)
  • [ ] Understand warranty/return policy
  • [ ] Verify VIN matches on all documents
  • [ ] Get everything in writing

Red Flags (Walk Away)

  • 🚩 Title not in seller's name
  • 🚩 Won't allow pre-purchase inspection
  • 🚩 "Too good to be true" price
  • 🚩 Fresh oil (hiding leaks/burning)
  • 🚩 Mismatched tires (neglect)
  • 🚩 Salvage/rebuilt title
  • 🚩 Pressure to buy immediately
---

Post-Purchase

Immediate Actions

1. Get insurance before driving home

2. Register vehicle (varies by state)

3. Change oil/filter (baseline maintenance)

4. Check all fluids, belts, hoses

5. Set up maintenance schedule

Maintenance Priorities

  • Timing belt (if due)
  • Transmission service
  • Brake pads/rotors inspection
  • Suspension check
  • Alignment

Summary: Success Formula

1. Research → Know the car, know the price

2. Inspect → Check everything, PPI mandatory

3. Negotiate → Focus on out-the-door price

4. Verify → All documents, clean title

5. Protect → Insurance, maintenance, records
